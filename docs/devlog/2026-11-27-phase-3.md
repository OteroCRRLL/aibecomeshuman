# Dev log: 2026-11-27, phase 3 (perception from recordings)

## What was done
- `aibh perceive <recording>`: keyframes (local), observer (Claude Haiku, vision),
  redaction, transcript, speakers and picks, draft scenario, evaluation against labels.
  `--dry-run` (free estimate), `--from/--to`, `--rebuild` (redo everything after the
  observer from saved observations, for under one cent).
- Local, free detectors: choice menus (coloured button letters in a column), new
  subtitles (white text with a dark outline) and new shots.
- Redaction: menus, flowchart, tutorial hints and configured texts never reach the agent.
  The "software instability" notice is hidden; the probability of success is kept.
- A scene taken from a recording can be played by the agent (`aibh play
  runs/<run>/scenario.toml`): it decides on its own at every recorded choice.
- Control panel: Recordings tab (estimate, analyse, rebuild, transcript with frame
  thumbnails, score).

## First recording (the first mission, 12 minutes, played by the operator)
- Reviewed by hand first (one frame every 4 s) to make labels.
- Final numbers: 10/10 choices detected, 8/8 picks, 19/19 lines, 14–15/15 speakers,
  0 leaks. ~$0.20 for the whole mission.
- Problems found and fixed on the way: every frame looked "changed" (the camera always
  moves), objective lists read as choices, an option unlocked by a clue read as locked,
  a 2-second timed menu missed between keyframes (fixed by the local menu detector),
  short subtitles missed, all-caps deductions read as dialogue, speakers guessed from the
  person on screen (usually the listener; fixed by the conversation pass).
- Claude Haiku playing the recorded mission as Connor: 12 decisions for $0.02, a
  different path from the operator's (calm instead of empathy, a lie, a compromise
  instead of the sacrifice).

## Known limitations
- Descriptions of fast action can be wrong (a fall read as "flying"). The judge
  (phase 4) will also check descriptions against what the agent may know.
- Stick choices (e.g. save the fish) have truncated labels and their pick is guessed.
- The time a menu stays on screen says how fast the operator answered, not the game's
  countdown; scenarios from recordings assume at least 6 s.
- The observer model may know this game; its outputs are checked only by the rules and
  labels so far (phase 4: anti-metagaming guard).
